1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Full names for structures (“internal carotid artery,” not “ICA”). Cranial nerves may be written as CN III or oculomotor nerve.
  • Use the lecture’s modality terms: special sensory (CN I, II, VIII, taste), general sensory, branchial motor.
  • Spelling counts: sagittal · cribriform · sternocleidomastoid · tensor veli palatini · carotid sheath · sphincter pupillae · superior orbital fissure · ptosis · hematoma.
★ High-yield traps
  1. Middle meningeal artery → EPIDURAL hematoma — it runs between the skull and the dura. Lens-shaped; does not cross sutures. Subdural = bridging veins.
  2. Dural venous sinuses form where the two dural layers (periosteal + meningeal) fail to fuse at the edges of dural folds.
  3. Falx = left/right. Tentorium = up/down. Falx cerebri anchors to the crista galli of the ethmoid.
  4. CN VI runs through the cavernous sinus (next to the internal carotid artery) → first affected. CN III, IV, V1, V2 are in the lateral wall. CN V3 is not in the sinus.
  5. Palatoglossus = CN X (the only tongue muscle not supplied by CN XII). Styloglossus = CN XII. Stylopharyngeus = CN IX.
  6. Light reflex: afferent CN II, efferent CN III. Corneal reflex: afferent CN V1, efferent CN VII. Gag: afferent CN IX, efferent CN X.
  7. CN VIII never leaves the skull — it ends in the inner ear within the petrous temporal bone.
  8. Tongue deviates toward a CN XII lesion; uvula deviates away from a CN X lesion; jaw deviates toward a CN V3 lesion.

2 · Meninges, Dural Folds & Dural Venous Sinuses

Coronal schematic, not to scale. Superficial → deep: skull · dura (periosteal + meningeal) · arachnoid · pia · brain.

Dura mater

Tough, “tarp-like”; attaches to the inside of the skull and holds the brain in place. Two layers: periosteal (outer) + meningeal (inner). Dura = durable.

Arachnoid mater

Delicate, thin, spider-web-like middle layer. Arachnoid = arachnid.

Pia mater

Soft, highly vascular; adheres to the brain and follows every contour — can’t be dissected away. Pia = pious, clings to the brain.

Outside → in: “DAP” — Dura, Arachnoid, Pia.

Dural venous sinuses

What to know
  • Formation: at the edges of dural folds the periosteal and meningeal layers don’t fuse → open channels for venous blood. Split the sheet → sinus.
  • They leave grooves on the inner skull (osteology list: grooves for the superior sagittal, transverse and sigmoid sinuses).
  • No valves → blood can flow in either direction → infection can travel back into the cranium (danger triangle, scalp emissary veins).
  • Sit between the dural layers (not in the subarachnoid space) and drain toward the internal jugular vein.

3 · Cavernous Sinus

CN V3 does not pass through the cavernous sinus — it leaves via the foramen ovale.

Contents

Through the sinus: internal carotid artery + sympathetic carotid plexus + CN VI

In the lateral wall (top → bottom): CN III → IV → V1 → V2

Receives: cerebral veins · ophthalmic veins · pterygoid plexus (emissary veins via foramen ovale / lacerum)

Cavernous sinus syndrome

  • Ophthalmoplegia — CN III, IV, VI
  • Ptosis — CN III + sympathetics
  • Absent pupillary response — CN III
  • Loss of corneal + maxillary sensation — CN V1, V2
  • Horner syndrome — sympathetic plexus
  • CN VI usually first (free in the sinus)

Routes of infection → thrombophlebitis

Facial vein→Ophthalmic veins→Cavernous sinus
Deep facial vein→Pterygoid plexus→Emissary veins→Cavernous sinus
Ethmoid / sphenoid sinusitis→Orbital veins→Cavernous sinus

Signs: lid swelling, proptosis, extraocular muscle impairment.

Pituitary tumor

Lateral extension into the cavernous sinus compresses CN VI, V1 and V2.

Surgical access: trans-nasal, trans-sphenoidal route through the sphenoid sinus.

8 · CN IX · X · XI · XII

CN IX — glossopharyngeal

  • 1 muscle: stylopharyngeus — elevates pharynx + larynx
  • 1 gland: parotid
  • Tongue: taste + general sensation, posterior 1/3; oropharynx sensation
  • Carotid sinus (blood pressure) + carotid body (O2/CO2/pH)
  • Afferent limb of gag
CN IX→Lesser petrosal→Otic ganglion→Auriculotemporal (V3)→Parotid

CN X — vagus

  • In the carotid sheath, posterior, between the carotid artery and internal jugular vein
  • Palate muscles except tensor veli palatini (CN V3)
  • Pharynx muscles except stylopharyngeus (CN IX)
  • Larynx muscles; palatoglossus
  • Lesion: uvula deviates away; recurrent laryngeal → hoarseness
  • Efferent limb of gag; taste from the epiglottis region

CN XI — accessory

  • Spinal root up through foramen magnum, out through jugular foramen
  • Sternocleidomastoid (turn head to opposite side) + trapezius (shrug)
  • Most exposed in the posterior triangle (lymph node biopsy) → usually trapezius only: shoulder droop, weak shrug

CN XII — hypoglossal

  • Hypoglossal canal (occipital bone)
  • All intrinsic + extrinsic tongue muscles except palatoglossus
  • Lesion: tongue deviates toward the lesion (opposite genioglossus unopposed)
  • Lateral to hyoglossus; lingual artery deep (medial) to it
  • Carries C1 fibers → thyrohyoid, geniohyoid, superior root of ansa cervicalis

11 · Self-Check Questions

Answer first, then open each one to check.

1. List the meninges from superficial to deep, and the two layers of the dura.
Dura (periosteal + meningeal layers) → arachnoid → pia.
2. How does a dural venous sinus form?
At the edges of dural folds the periosteal and meningeal layers don’t fuse, leaving an open venous channel.
3. What does the falx cerebri separate, and where does it anchor anteriorly?
Left and right cerebral hemispheres; the crista galli of the ethmoid bone.
4. What does the tentorium cerebelli separate?
Cerebrum (above) from cerebellum (below).
5. Which nerve runs through the cavernous sinus rather than in its wall, and why does that matter?
CN VI — it lies free next to the internal carotid artery and is usually affected first.
6. A pterion fracture tears which artery, causing which hematoma, with what CT shape?
Middle meningeal artery → epidural hematoma → biconvex/lens-shaped, doesn’t cross sutures.
7. Through which opening does the internal carotid artery enter the skull, and which branch goes to the orbit?
Carotid canal; the ophthalmic artery, through the optic canal with CN II.
8. Which three nerves and which vein pass through the jugular foramen?
CN IX, X, XI and the internal jugular vein (continuation of the sigmoid sinus).
9. Which tongue muscle is not supplied by CN XII?
Palatoglossus — CN X.
10. Name the afferent and efferent limbs of the light, corneal and gag reflexes.
Light: II → III. Corneal: V1 → VII. Gag: IX → X.
11. Where is the sensory ganglion of CN V?
Trigeminal ganglion, middle cranial fossa.
12. A pituitary tumor extends laterally. Which nerves are compressed?
CN VI, V1 and V2 (in the cavernous sinus).
