Lab 1 Exam-Day Review — The Neck

Dissection of the Neck — fascia and spaces, triangles, cervical plexus, suprahyoid and infrahyoid muscles, carotid sheath, vagal branches, larynx landmarks, thyroid gland and the root of the neck.

1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Full names, anatomical directions. Spelling counts: sternocleidomastoid · omohyoid · sternothyroid · thyrohyoid · ansa cervicalis · thyrocervical · costocervical · cricothyroid · supraclavicular · great auricular.
★ High-yield traps
  1. Ansa cervicalis (C1–C3) supplies sternohyoid, sternothyroid, omohyoid. Thyrohyoid = C1 via CN XII (not the ansa).
  2. Carotid sheath: common carotid medial, internal jugular vein lateral, vagus posterior between them. Deep cervical nodes follow the internal jugular vein.
  3. Anterior scalene: phrenic nerve + subclavian vein anterior to it; subclavian artery + brachial plexus trunks posterior (interscalene triangle).
  4. Suprahyoid nerves: stylohyoid + posterior digastric = CN VII; mylohyoid + anterior digastric = V3.
  5. Superior thyroid artery ↔ external laryngeal nerve; superior laryngeal artery ↔ internal laryngeal nerve; inferior thyroid artery ↔ recurrent laryngeal nerve.
  6. Cricothyroid is the only intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the external laryngeal nerve; all others = recurrent laryngeal.
  7. Carotid body (chemoreceptor) + carotid sinus (baroreceptor) = CN IX.
  8. Right recurrent laryngeal loops the right subclavian artery; left loops the aortic arch.

2 · Surface Anatomy, Fascia & Spaces

Surface landmarks

  • Mental protuberance — chin
  • Laryngeal prominence — Adam’s apple (thyroid cartilage)
  • Jugular notch — lowest palpable midline landmark (manubrium)
  • Mastoid process — posteroinferior to the ear; SCM attachment
  • SCM divides the neck into anterior and posterior triangles
  • Anterior border of trapezius — posterior boundary of the posterior triangle

Fascial layers

  • Superficial fascia: cutaneous nerves, vessels, lymphatics, fat, platysma
  • Investing: 360° collar; splits around SCM and trapezius
  • Pretracheal: thyroid, trachea, esophagus; posteriorly = buccopharyngeal fascia
  • Carotid sheath: common carotid, internal jugular vein, vagus
  • Prevertebral: vertebrae + deep neck muscles (encloses the phrenic nerve)
  • Alar: subdivides the retropharyngeal space

CN XI injury (posterior triangle)

  • Ipsilateral shoulder droop, weak shrug
  • Weak head turn to the opposite side against resistance
  • Difficulty abducting the arm above 90°

Exits the jugular foramen → SCM → crosses the posterior triangle superficially → trapezius.

Torticollis (wry neck)

Contracted SCM → head tilts toward the affected side, chin points to the opposite side. Congenital (SCM fibrosis, birth trauma) or spasmodic (cervical dystonia).

★ Referred shoulder pain
Diaphragm irritation → pain felt over the shoulder: phrenic and supraclavicular nerves both arise from C3–C5.

Superficial veins

  • External jugular vein = posterior auricular + retromandibular (posterior division); descends superficial to SCM → subclavian vein. Superficial cervical nodes follow it. JVD in right heart failure.
  • Anterior jugular vein — anterior neck → external jugular vein.

Deep vein

  • Internal jugular vein — lateral in the carotid sheath; + subclavian → brachiocephalic vein.
  • Tributaries: common facial vein, superior + middle thyroid veins.
  • Central line site (with the subclavian vein). Deep cervical nodes run along it.

★ C1 hitchhikers on CN XII
C1 fibers leave CN XII as (1) the superior root of the ansa, (2) the nerve to thyrohyoid, (3) the nerve to geniohyoid. A tag on thyrohyoid → answer C1 via CN XII, not the ansa.

CN XII in the neck

Hypoglossal canal → descends between ICA and IJV → hooks forward superficial to the internal + external carotid arteries → carotid + submandibular triangles (deep to posterior digastric) → tongue. Gives off C1 fibers (superior root of the ansa, nerve to thyrohyoid).

Cricothyroid muscle

External to the larynx, between the cricoid and thyroid cartilages. Tilts the thyroid cartilage → lengthens/tenses the vocal folds → raises pitch. Nerve: external laryngeal.

9 · Thyroid & Parathyroid Glands

Anatomy

  • Anterolateral to larynx/trachea, deep to the strap muscles, in pretracheal fascia
  • Right + left lobes joined by the isthmus
  • Pyramidal lobe (variable) = remnant of the thyroglossal duct
  • 4 parathyroids on the posterior surface

Thyroidectomy danger

  • Inferior thyroid artery ↔ recurrent laryngeal nerve → hoarseness
  • Superior thyroid artery ↔ external laryngeal nerve → lost high pitch
  • Horner syndrome (sympathetic trunk) is also possible

10 · Root of the Neck

Side view. Anterior to the anterior scalene: phrenic nerve and subclavian vein. Posterior to it (interscalene triangle, above the 1st rib): subclavian artery and brachial plexus trunks.

Anterior scalene syndrome (thoracic outlet)

Cervical rib, hypertrophied scalenes or trauma compress the subclavian artery + brachial plexus in the interscalene triangle → vascular + neurologic upper limb symptoms. The subclavian vein is spared (anterior to the muscle).

Phrenic nerve

C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive. Descends on the anterior scalene within prevertebral fascia → posterior to the subclavian vein, anterior to the subclavian artery → thorax. Motor to the diaphragm; sensory to fibrous pericardium + parietal pleura.

Right subclavian = brachiocephalic trunk; left = aortic arch. Continues past the 1st rib as the axillary artery.

Lymph ducts

  • Thoracic duct — left side; enters the left venous angle (IJV + subclavian)
  • Right lymphatic duct — right venous angle
  • Jugular trunks carry all head/neck lymph (deep cervical nodes along the IJV) to these ducts

Brachial plexus & scalenes

  • Plexus = ventral rami C5–T1; trunks emerge between anterior + middle scalenes
  • Anterior + middle scalene → 1st rib; posterior scalene → 2nd rib; all = cervical ventral rami
  • Posterior triangle floor: splenius capitis (dorsal rami), levator scapulae (dorsal scapular n.), scalenes

11 · Cervical Sympathetic Trunk & Horner Syndrome

Upper thoracic cord (preganglionic)→Ascend the sympathetic trunk (posterior to carotid sheath)→Synapse: superior / middle / inferior cervical ganglia→Postganglionic fibers ride the carotid arteries to the head

Inferior cervical ganglion often fuses with the 1st thoracic → stellate ganglion.

★ Horner syndrome — “PAM Horner”
Ptosis (mild; superior tarsal muscle) · Anhidrosis · Miosis (dilator pupillae lost). Causes: Pancoast tumor at the lung apex, penetrating neck trauma, surgery (thyroidectomy, carotid endarterectomy).

12 · Self-Check

1. What are the boundaries of the carotid triangle?
Posterior belly of digastric, superior belly of omohyoid, anterior border of SCM.
2. Which infrahyoid muscle is NOT supplied by the ansa cervicalis?
Thyrohyoid — C1 fibers traveling with CN XII.
3. Arrange the carotid sheath contents.
Common carotid artery medial, internal jugular vein lateral, vagus posterior between them.
4. Which structures pass anterior vs. posterior to the anterior scalene?
Anterior: phrenic nerve, subclavian vein (and transverse cervical + suprascapular arteries crossing). Posterior: subclavian artery, brachial plexus trunks.
5. A singer can no longer hit high notes after thyroid surgery. Which nerve, and which artery does it travel with?
External laryngeal nerve (cricothyroid); superior thyroid artery.
6. Loss of the cough reflex — which nerve?
Internal laryngeal nerve (sensory above the vocal folds; afferent limb).
7. Where does the left recurrent laryngeal nerve loop?
Around the aortic arch (right loops the right subclavian artery).
8. Which nerve innervates the carotid body and sinus?
CN IX (glossopharyngeal).
9. A patient cannot shrug the right shoulder after a posterior triangle lymph node biopsy. Which nerve?
Right CN XI (accessory).
10. Why does diaphragm irritation cause shoulder pain?
Phrenic and supraclavicular nerves share C3–C5.
11. What are the branches of the thyrocervical trunk?
Inferior thyroid, transverse cervical, suprascapular arteries.
12. Where is a cricothyroidotomy performed?
Through the cricothyroid membrane, between the thyroid and cricoid cartilages.
13. Which fascial space spreads infection all the way to the diaphragm?
The danger space (between alar and prevertebral fascia).
14. Which nerves supply the stylohyoid and the anterior belly of the digastric?
Stylohyoid = CN VII; anterior digastric = nerve to mylohyoid (V3).
15. Where does the common carotid bifurcate?
At the superior border of the thyroid cartilage.
