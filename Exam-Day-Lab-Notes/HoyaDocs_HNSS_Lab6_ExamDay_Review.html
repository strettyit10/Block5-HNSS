Lab 6 Exam-Day Review — Palate & Oral Cavity

Dissection of the Palate and Oral Cavity — hard and soft palate, tonsillar pillars, nasopharynx lateral wall, tongue muscles, floor of the mouth, salivary glands, ganglia and neurovasculature.

1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Use full names and anatomical directions. Spelling counts: palatine aponeurosis · pterygoid hamulus · oropharyngeal isthmus · epiglottic vallecula · salpingopharyngeus · stylopharyngeus · hypoglossal · mylohyoid · geniohyoid · incisive foramen.
  • Action verbs matter: tensor = tenses, levator = elevates. No palate muscle “thickens.”
★ High-yield traps
  1. Pharyngotympanic tube = middle ear ↔ nasopharynx (not the inner ear, not the outside).
  2. Soft palate muscles = CN X (pharyngeal plexus) except tensor veli palatini = CN V3. Tongue muscles = CN XII except palatoglossus = CN X.
  3. Hyoglossus sandwich: CN XII superficial, lingual artery deep → the muscle is the landmark for finding the artery.
  4. CN XII lesion → tongue points TOWARD the lesion. CN X lesion → uvula points AWAY.
  5. Tonsillectomy: CN IX deep to the tonsillar bed (taste/sensation posterior ⅓ + ear pain). Rare but serious: internal carotid artery.
  6. Geniohyoid = C1 riding with CN XII, not CN XII. Mylohyoid + anterior digastric = V3 (nerve to mylohyoid).
  7. Molar roots (2nd/3rd) lie below the mylohyoid line → submandibular space → Ludwig’s angina.
  8. Greater palatine foramen is in the palatine bone. Anterior hard palate = nasopalatine nerve via the incisive foramen.

Epiglottic vallecula

  • Pocket between the posterior ⅓ of the tongue (anterior) and the epiglottis (posterior); paired, split by the median glosso-epiglottic fold.
  • Oropharynx.
  • Intubation: laryngoscope blade sits here → lifts the tongue + epiglottis → exposes the laryngeal inlet.

Mallampati score

  • Size of the tongue relative to the oral cavity (how much oropharynx is visible).
  • High score → difficult intubation → plan a video laryngoscope.

Epiglottis

  • Elastic cartilage; seals the laryngeal inlet during swallowing (larynx rises, tongue base pushes it down).
  • Its superior border = oropharynx / laryngopharynx boundary.

Vallecula vs. piriform recess

ValleculaAnterior to the epiglottis · oropharynx · laryngoscope blade
Piriform recessLateral to the laryngeal inlet · laryngopharynx · fish bones → internal laryngeal nerve

Same origin (pharyngotympanic tube cartilage), different path: the hamulus turns the tensor into a horizontal tightener; the levator pulls straight up.

CN X lesion — uvula

Right CN X lesion → right levator paralyzed → healthy left levator pulls → uvula deviates to the left (away from the lesion).

Soft palate at a glance

  • Posterior ⅓ of the palate; ends as the uvula
  • Job: elevates → seals the nasopharynx (no nasal regurgitation; no nasal air escape in speech)
  • Sensation: lesser palatine nerve (V2) + CN IX posteriorly

Open-mouth view. The oropharyngeal isthmus is the gap between the two palatoglossal (anterior) folds — the doorway from the oral cavity into the oropharynx.

★ Tonsillectomy
Layers outward: palatine tonsil → tonsillar bed → superior pharyngeal constrictor → CN IX → internal carotid artery.
  • CN IX injury: temporary loss of taste + general sensation to the posterior ⅓ of the tongue and ear pain (referred otalgia — CN IX also supplies the middle ear).
  • Rare but serious: internal carotid artery. (Tonsillar branch of the facial artery = routine bleeding.)

6 · Nasopharynx Lateral Wall & Pharyngotympanic Tube

Opening of pharyngotympanic tube→Torus tubarius (tube cartilage)→Pharyngeal recess

Anterior → posterior on the lateral wall. The salpingopharyngeal fold (salpingopharyngeus) runs inferiorly from the torus; the pharyngeal tonsil (adenoids) sits on the roof/posterior wall.

Muscles that open the tube

  • Tensor veli palatini — V3
  • Levator veli palatini — CN X
  • Salpingopharyngeus — CN X

Swallowing on a plane → tube opens → middle ear pressure equalizes.

Clinical

  • Adenoid hypertrophy blocks the tube opening → poor middle ear ventilation → effusion → otitis media. Children: tube shorter + more horizontal.
  • Nasopharyngeal tumor (pharyngeal recess) → tube blocked → unilateral effusion + conductive hearing loss in an adult.
Longitudinal pharyngeal muscleOriginNerveSpecial action
StylopharyngeusStyloid processCN IX (only muscle; 3rd arch)Elevates + widens the pharynx
PalatopharyngeusSoft palateCN XElevates + shortens
SalpingopharyngeusTube cartilage (salpingo- = tube)CN XElevates; helps open the tube

“Style is 9, the rest are 10.” All three elevate the pharynx during swallowing.

Genioglossus & the airway

Unconscious + supine → genioglossus relaxes → tongue falls posteriorly → blocks the oropharynx. Jaw thrust / chin lift pulls the mandible (its origin) forward → tongue follows.

8 · Floor of the Mouth

Layers (oral side → skin)

Genioglossus↓Geniohyoid (C1 via CN XII)↓MYLOHYOID = the floor (V3)↓Anterior belly of digastric (V3)↓Platysma → skin

Ludwig’s angina

  • Rapidly spreading infection of the submandibular, sublingual and submental spaces
  • Source: 2nd/3rd mandibular molars — roots below the mylohyoid line
  • Pus pushes the tongue up and back → airway obstruction
  • Toothache, trouble speaking/swallowing, swollen tongue, firm swollen neck

9 · Hyoglossus Region — Telling Structures Apart

Lateral view of the left floor of the mouth. On the hyoglossus, superior → inferior: lingual nerve, submandibular duct, CN XII. The lingual artery is hidden medial (deep) to the muscle.
★ Hyoglossus sandwich
CN XII (+ lingual nerve + submandibular duct) lateral · lingual artery medial (deep). Find the hyoglossus, retract it, and the artery is underneath → ligated for base-of-tongue cancer resection or used for microvascular reconstruction. Lingual artery = 3rd branch of the external carotid (SALFOPMS); may share a linguofacial trunk with the facial artery.

Submandibular ganglion (parasympathetic)

CN VII→Chorda tympani→Lingual nerve (V3)→Submandibular ganglion (synapse)→Submandibular + sublingual glands

“Chorda → Chomp → Chin glands.”

Pterygopalatine ganglion (parasympathetic)

CN VII→Greater petrosal nerve→Pterygopalatine ganglion (synapse)→Lacrimal + nasal, palatal, sinus, nasopharyngeal glands

In the pterygopalatine fossa, hanging below V2. V2 sensory fibers (greater/lesser palatine, nasopalatine) pass through without synapsing.

LesionLostSpared
Greater petrosal nerveDry eye; dry nose/palate; taste from the soft palateFace, hearing, tongue taste, saliva

“Tears, Ears, Taste, Face” = CN VII branches in the order they leave (greater petrosal → nerve to stapedius → chorda tympani → motor branches). A single-branch lesion loses only that branch.

Sialolithiasis → sialadenitis

  • Stone blocks the duct (usually submandibular: thicker, calcium-rich saliva; long duct running uphill)
  • Mucopurulent drainage, facial pain + swelling, foul taste
  • Antibiotics, hydration, sialogogues, sour candy/drinks
  • Stone removal risks the lingual nerve (loops under the duct)
“Buccal” trap: buccal branch of CN VII = motor to the buccinator. Buccal nerve of V3 = sensory to the cheek (passes through the buccinator without supplying it).

11 · Mandible, Inferior Alveolar & Lingual Nerves

CN V3→Inferior alveolar nerve→(nerve to mylohyoid leaves)→Mandibular foramen (lingula)→Mandibular canal → lower teeth→Mental foramen → mental nerve (chin, lower lip)

Inferior alveolar nerve block

Anesthetic at the mandibular foramen (medial ramus). The lingual nerve lies just anterior/medial in the same space → also numbed → anterior ⅔ of the tongue goes numb too.

Upper teeth = posterior, middle and anterior superior alveolar nerves (V2) → anesthetic must diffuse to several nerves.

Lingual nerve injury (3rd molar extraction)

Lies in soft tissue only against the lingual side of the 3rd molar (the inferior alveolar nerve is protected in the mandibular canal).

  • Loss of general sensation, anterior ⅔ of the tongue
  • Loss of taste (chorda tympani)
  • ↓ salivation (submandibular + sublingual)

Lymph drainage of the tongue & lips

Anterior ⅓ tongue · medial lower lipSubmental nodes → deep cervical
Lateral tongueSubmandibular + jugulodigastric nodes
Upper lip · lateral lower lipSubmandibular nodes → deep cervical
Posterior ⅓ tongueDeep cervical nodes directly (can spread down the internal jugular vein)

12 · Self-Check

1. What does the pharyngotympanic tube connect?
The middle ear (tympanic cavity) and the nasopharynx.
2. Which soft palate muscle is not supplied by CN X, and what is its nerve?
Tensor veli palatini — CN V3 (1st pharyngeal arch).
3. What bony hook does the tensor veli palatini tendon wrap around, and what does the tendon become?
The pterygoid hamulus; the palatine aponeurosis.
4. A patient’s uvula deviates to the right. Which nerve, which side?
Left CN X (the healthy right levator pulls the uvula to the right).
5. The tongue deviates to the left on protrusion. Which nerve, which side?
Left CN XII (the healthy right genioglossus pushes the tongue to the left).
6. What is the opening between the two palatoglossal folds?
The oropharyngeal isthmus — from the oral cavity into the oropharynx.
7. Which nerve is at risk deep to the tonsillar bed, and why does injury cause ear pain?
CN IX; it also supplies the middle ear, so the pain is referred to the ear.
8. Which artery lies deep to the hyoglossus, and which nerve lies superficial to it?
Lingual artery (deep); CN XII (superficial) — the “hyoglossus sandwich.”
9. What innervates the geniohyoid?
C1 fibers traveling with CN XII.
10. Why does a 3rd molar abscess cause Ludwig’s angina?
Its roots lie below the mylohyoid line, so pus drains into the submandibular space and spreads to the sublingual and submental spaces.
11. Where does the parotid duct open? The sublingual ducts?
Parotid: oral vestibule opposite the maxillary 2nd molar. Sublingual: many small ducts lateral to the frenulum.
12. Which foramen transmits the greater palatine nerve, and which bone is it in?
Greater palatine foramen, in the palatine bone.
13. Where does the laryngoscope blade go, and in which part of the pharynx is it?
The epiglottic vallecula, in the oropharynx.
14. A greater petrosal nerve lesion causes what?
Dry eye, dry nose/palate glands, loss of soft palate taste — no facial paralysis.
