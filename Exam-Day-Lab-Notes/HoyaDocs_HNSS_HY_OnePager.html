Lesion directions

  • Tongue → TOWARD CN XII lesion (healthy genioglossus pushes)
  • Uvula → AWAY from CN X lesion (healthy levator veli palatini pulls)
  • Jaw → TOWARD CN V3 lesion (healthy lateral pterygoid pushes)
  • CN XI: shoulder droop, weak shrug, weak turn to opposite side
  • Torticollis: head tilts to affected SCM, chin to opposite side

Reflexes (afferent → efferent)

  • Light: II → III (parasympathetic, ciliary ganglion → sphincter pupillae)
  • Corneal: V1 (nasociliary) → VII (bilateral blink)
  • Gag: IX → X · Cough: internal laryngeal (X) afferent
  • Carotid sinus (baro) + carotid body (chemo) = IX

CN VII branch order — “Tears, Ears, Taste, Face”

  • Greater petrosal → pterygopalatine ggl → lacrimal + nose/palate glands
  • Nerve to stapedius → hyperacusis if lost
  • Chorda tympani → lingual n. → submandibular ggl → submandibular + sublingual; taste ant ⅔ (never the lacrimal gland)
  • Stylomastoid foramen → face only. A single-branch lesion loses only that branch.
  • Lesser petrosal (IX) → otic ggl → auriculotemporal → parotid

Neck

  • Carotid sheath: CCA medial · IJV lateral · X posterior; ansa anterior; sympathetic trunk posterior. Deep cervical nodes on IJV; superficial on EJV.
  • CCA bifurcates at superior border of thyroid cartilage
  • Anterior scalene: phrenic (C3–5) + subclavian vein anterior; subclavian artery + plexus trunks posterior (interscalene △)
  • Subclavian “VIT C D”: vertebral, internal thoracic, thyrocervical (inf. thyroid, transverse cervical, suprascapular) · costocervical · dorsal scapular
  • ECA “SALFOPMS”: sup. thyroid, asc. pharyngeal, lingual, facial, occipital, post. auricular, maxillary, superficial temporal
  • Triangles: carotid = post. digastric + sup. omohyoid + SCM; submandibular = mandible + 2 digastric bellies; posterior = SCM + trapezius + clavicle (CN XI, Erb’s point)
  • Danger space (alar ↔ prevertebral) → diaphragm; retropharyngeal → T1–T2
  • Diaphragm pain → shoulder (phrenic + supraclavicular = C3–5)
  • Horner “PAM”: ptosis (superior tarsal), anhidrosis, miosis — Pancoast, neck trauma, thyroid/carotid surgery
★ Artery–nerve pairs
  • Superior thyroid a. ↔ external laryngeal n. (lose high pitch)
  • Superior laryngeal a. ↔ internal laryngeal n. (pierce thyrohyoid membrane; cough reflex)
  • Inferior thyroid a. ↔ recurrent laryngeal n. (hoarseness; thyroidectomy)
  • Deep temporal a. ↔ deep temporal n.; superficial temporal a. ↔ auriculotemporal n.
  • Auriculotemporal n. splits around the middle meningeal a.; lingual n. loops under the submandibular duct
  • Palate: nasopalatine ↔ sphenopalatine (incisive) · greater palatine n. ↔ a. · lesser palatine n. ↔ a.
  • Orbit: every artery = ophthalmic branch, named like its nerve

Larynx

  • PCA = only abductor (“Pulls Cords Apart”) — RLN. Bilateral RLN → stridor.
  • Cricothyroid tenses → ↑ pitch (external laryngeal); thyroarytenoid relaxes → ↓ pitch
  • Vocal fold = dividing line: above = internal laryngeal, sup. laryngeal a., superior deep cervical nodes · below = RLN, inf. laryngeal a., inferior deep cervical + pre/paratracheal. Vocal folds: sparse lymph → rare mets.
  • Vestibular (false) fold = quadrangular membrane · vocal (true) = conus elasticus · ventricle between
  • RLN loops: R subclavian a. / L aortic arch
  • Cricothyroidotomy = cricothyroid ligament · tracheostomy = between rings
  • Epiglottis = elastic cartilage; seals the laryngeal inlet

Pharynx & swallowing

  • Nasopharynx lateral wall: tube opening → torus tubarius → pharyngeal recess (NPC hides here)
  • Pharyngotympanic tube = MIDDLE ear ↔ nasopharynx; opened by TVP (V3), LVP + salpingopharyngeus (X). Kids: short, horizontal → otitis media.
  • Vallecula = oropharynx, laryngoscope blade · piriform recess = laryngopharynx, fish bone → internal laryngeal n.
  • Longitudinal: stylo (IX, widens), palato (shortens), salpingo — all elevate
  • Killian’s triangle (thyropharyngeus/cricopharyngeus) → Zenker’s
  • Retropharyngeal space: sympathetic trunk + CN IX–XII near the skull base; NPC compresses them

Tonsil / oropharynx

  • Anterior pillar = palatoglossal fold · posterior = palatopharyngeal · tonsil between
  • Oropharyngeal isthmus = between the palatoglossal folds
  • Tonsillectomy: CN IX deep to bed → taste/sensation post ⅓ + referred ear pain; rare but serious = ICA
★ Palate
  • Hard = palatine process of maxilla + horizontal plate of palatine
  • Incisive (maxilla): nasopalatine · greater palatine (palatine bone, 3rd molar): greater palatine n./a. · lesser palatine: soft palate
  • TVP hooks the pterygoid hamulus → palatine aponeurosis → TENSES; LVP ELEVATES (seals nasopharynx)
  • Adenoid cystic carcinoma → perineural spread along greater palatine n. → PPF → skull

Tongue & floor of mouth

  • Genio = protrude · hyo = retrude + depress · stylo = retrude + elevate · palato (X) = elevate posterior
  • Hyoglossus sandwich: XII (+ lingual n., duct) lateral · lingual a. deep → ligate for tongue-base cancer / free flap
  • On hyoglossus sup→inf: lingual n. (ganglion hangs) → duct → XII
  • Mylohyoid line: 2nd/3rd molar roots below → submandibular space → Ludwig’s angina (airway)
  • Lingual n. at 3rd molar (soft tissue): lose sensation + taste ant ⅔ + saliva
  • Unconscious: tongue falls back → jaw thrust
  • Tongue lymph: tip → submental · lateral → submandibular/jugulodigastric · posterior → deep cervical

Face & infratemporal fossa

  • Danger triangle: facial v. → angular v. → superior ophthalmic v. → cavernous sinus (valveless) → CN VI first
  • SCALP: skin, connective, aponeurosis (epicranial), loose CT (danger layer), pericranium
  • Maxillary a. 1st part: middle meningeal (foramen spinosum), inferior alveolar · 3rd: sphenopalatine, descending palatine, infraorbital
  • Mental foramen below lower 2nd premolar · IAN block at mandibular foramen also numbs lingual n.
  • Lateral pterygoid = only opener (protrudes); others close
  • Buccal branch VII = buccinator (motor) ≠ buccal n. V3 (sensory)

Cranial cavity

  • Middle meningeal a. → EPIDURAL (lens, pterion); bridging veins → subdural
  • Sinuses = where dural layers don’t fuse; valveless · falx = L/R; tentorium = up/down
  • Cavernous sinus: VI + ICA inside; III, IV, V1, V2 in the lateral wall; V3 not
  • Hutchinson sign: shingles on nose tip (external nasal, V1) → eye involvement

Orbit

  • H-test: abduct → test recti; adduct → test obliques. SR+IR adduct; SO+IO abduct; “superiors intort”
  • CN III palsy: down & out, ptosis, dilated pupil · IV: can’t look down when adducted · VI: can’t abduct
  • Only parasympathetics synapse in the ciliary ggl; long ciliary (nasociliary) → dilator
  • Tears: gland → canaliculi → lacrimal sac → nasolacrimal duct → inferior meatus
  • Lacrimal gland: VII → greater petrosal → PPF ggl → zygomatic (V2) → lacrimal (V1)
★ Nose & sinuses
  • Superior + middle conchae = ethmoid; inferior = own bone
  • Sphenoethmoidal recess ← sphenoid · superior meatus ← posterior ethmoid · middle meatus ← frontal, anterior + middle ethmoid, maxillary (semilunar hiatus) → sinusitis · inferior meatus ← nasolacrimal only
  • Septum: septal cartilage, perpendicular plate (ethmoid), vomer · DPH
  • Kiesselbach (anterior, most bleeds) vs sphenopalatine (maxillary a., posterior, serious)
  • Anterosuperior = V1 (anterior ethmoidal) · posteroinferior = V2 (nasopalatine, posterior nasal)
  • Piriform aperture anterior (maxilla + nasal bones) · choanae posterior
  • Blowout: floor → maxillary sinus, trapped IR (no upgaze), infraorbital numbness, enophthalmos
  • Lymph: anterior nose → submandibular; posterior + sinuses → retropharyngeal → superior deep cervical

Lymph

  • Everything ends in deep cervical nodes (IJV) → jugular trunk → thoracic duct (L) / right lymphatic duct (R)
  • Superficial cervical (EJV) ← occipital, mastoid, parotid, submandibular/submental
  • Omohyoid splits superior vs inferior deep cervical; jugulodigastric = tonsillar node
