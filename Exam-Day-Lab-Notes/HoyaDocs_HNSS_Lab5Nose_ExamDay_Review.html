Lab 5 Exam-Day Review — The Nose

Dissection of the Nasal Cavity — external nose, septum, lateral wall, paranasal sinus drainage, blood supply, innervation and blowout fractures.

1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Use anatomical directions (anterior/posterior, superior/inferior). “Conchae” and “turbinates” are the same thing.
  • Spelling counts: sphenoethmoidal recess · semilunar hiatus · ethmoidal bulla · frontonasal duct · nasolacrimal duct · perpendicular plate · vomer · alar · cribriform · Kiesselbach’s plexus · piriform aperture.
★ High-yield traps
  1. Superior + middle conchae = ethmoid bone; the inferior concha is its own bone.
  2. Middle meatus drains three sinuses (frontal, maxillary, ethmoidal) → blockage here is the most important cause of sinusitis.
  3. Inferior meatus = nasolacrimal duct only. No sinus drains there.
  4. Sphenoid sinus → sphenoethmoidal recess; posterior ethmoidal cells → superior meatus.
  5. Anterior (piriform) aperture is in front; the choana (posterior nasal aperture) is at the back.
  6. Kiesselbach’s plexus (anterior septum) = most nosebleeds; sphenopalatine artery (from the maxillary artery) = life-threatening posterior nosebleeds.
  7. Anterosuperior = V1 (anterior ethmoidal nerve); posteroinferior = V2 (nasopalatine on the septum, posterior nasal nerves laterally).
  8. Orbital floor blowout → contents drop into the maxillary sinus.

3 · Nasal Septum

Medial wall (septum), schematic. Anterosuperior septum = anterior ethmoidal nerve (V1); posteroinferior = nasopalatine nerve (V2).

Deviated septum

Shifted off center; treated only if obstruction is significant.

Septal perforation

Hole in the septum — drug use, surgery, autoimmune destruction.

Septal hematoma

Blood within the mucous membrane → pressure compromises blood supply + infection risk.

Septal pathology “DPH”: Deviated · Perforated · Hematoma.

Middle meatus landmarks

  • Ethmoidal bulla — rounded bulge formed by the middle ethmoidal cells
  • Semilunar hiatus — curved groove; superior border = bulla, inferior border = ethmoid bone
  • Maxillary opening in its floor; frontonasal duct at its anterosuperior end

“FAME drains in the middle”: Frontal · Anterior ethmoidal · Maxillary · middle Ethmoidal.

Lab tips

  • Lift or remove the middle concha to see the bulla and hiatus.
  • Follow the hiatus anterosuperiorly toward the frontal sinus → frontonasal duct.
  • Probes from the maxillary sinus exit lower and further back in the hiatus floor.
  • Conchae increase surface area and warm/humidify air.

6 · Blood Supply & Nosebleeds

Arteries (dual ICA + ECA supply)

  • Ophthalmic (ICA) → anterior + posterior ethmoidal arteries
  • Maxillary (ECA) → sphenopalatine artery (largest to the lateral wall), greater palatine artery
  • Facial (ECA) → superior labial artery (septal branch)

Veins & lymph

  • Ophthalmic veins → cavernous sinus (valveless “danger zone”)
  • Facial vein → internal jugular vein
  • Sphenopalatine vein → pterygoid plexus
  • Lymph: anterior → submandibular nodes · posterior + sinuses → retropharyngeal → upper deep cervical nodes

“Front-top = V1, back-bottom = V2.” “Smell = I, feel = V, secrete = VII.” V1 enters from the orbit (ethmoidal foramina); V2 enters from behind (sphenopalatine foramen).

8 · Orbital Blowout Fractures & the Sinuses

Floor fractureMedial wall fracture
Blows intoMaxillary sinusEthmoidal air cells
NerveInfraorbital (V2) → numb lower lid, cheek, upper lip, side of nose—
GlobeHypoglobus (sits low) / enophthalmos (sunken back)Enophthalmos
MovementTrapped inferior rectus → can’t look upTrapped medial rectus → can’t look out toward the injured side

Enophthalmos

Eye sunken back — orbital contents escape into a sinus.

Proptosis / exophthalmos

Eye bulges forward — something occupies space behind it (e.g., cavernous sinus thrombosis).

Hypoglobus

Eye sits lower — tissue drops into the maxillary sinus.

Paralyzed muscle loses its own action; trapped muscle blocks the opposite action. Don’t confuse proptosis (globe forward) with ptosis (drooping lid).

10 · Self-Check Questions

Answer first, then open each one to check.

1. Which conchae belong to the ethmoid bone?
Superior and middle conchae. The inferior concha is its own bone.
2. What drains into the sphenoethmoidal recess?
The sphenoid sinus.
3. What drains into the superior meatus?
Posterior ethmoidal cells.
4. List everything that drains into the middle meatus and how.
Frontal sinus + anterior ethmoidal cells (frontonasal duct); maxillary sinus (floor of the semilunar hiatus); middle ethmoidal cells (ethmoidal bulla).
5. What is the only structure that opens into the inferior meatus?
The nasolacrimal duct.
6. Name the three components of the nasal septum.
Septal cartilage (anterior), perpendicular plate of the ethmoid (posterosuperior), vomer (posteroinferior).
7. What is the other name for the anterior nasal aperture, and which bone borders it laterally and inferiorly?
Piriform aperture; the maxilla.
8. Which nerve supplies the posteroinferior septum?
Nasopalatine nerve (V2).
9. Which nerve supplies the anterosuperior nasal cavity (both walls)?
Anterior ethmoidal nerve (V1).
10. Where do most nosebleeds come from?
Kiesselbach’s plexus on the anterior septum.
11. Which sinus is the largest, and what forms its roof?
Maxillary sinus; the orbital floor (with the infraorbital nerve).
12. What is enophthalmos?
Posterior displacement (sinking) of the eyeball into the orbit.
