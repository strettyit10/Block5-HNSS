1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Write it the way the key is written
  • Use full names: “inferior alveolar nerve,” not “IAN” or “inf. alv. n.” Cranial nerves are the exception (CN V3 or mandibular nerve).
  • CN V3 splits into divisions (anterior / posterior). Those divisions give off branches.
  • CN VII branches are named “[name] branch of CN VII”. “Buccal nerve” on its own means the CN V3 sensory nerve.
  • Spelling on the practical counts: alveolar · orbicularis oculi / oris · pterion · infratemporal · lingual · zygomaticus · epicranial aponeurosis.
★ High-yield traps
  1. Chorda tympani → submandibular ganglion → submandibular + sublingual glands. It never supplies the lacrimal gland (that is the greater petrosal nerve).
  2. Danger triangle: facial vein → angular vein → superior ophthalmic vein → cavernous sinus. CN VI is affected first.
  3. Mental foramen sits below the lower 2nd premolar. The parotid duct opens opposite the upper 2nd molar.
  4. Epicranial aponeurosis (galea aponeurotica) is SCALP layer 3. The dangerous layer is layer 4, loose areolar tissue.
  5. Zygomaticus major and minor = zygomatic + buccal branches of CN VII.
  6. Platysma sits in the neck but is a muscle of facial expression (arch 2) → cervical branch of CN VII.
  7. CN VII passes through the parotid gland but does not supply it. Secretomotor = CN IX → otic ganglion → auriculotemporal nerve.
  8. Buccinator: motor = buccal branch of CN VII; sensation over it = buccal nerve (CN V3).
  9. Anterior belly of digastric = CN V3 (nerve to mylohyoid). Posterior belly = CN VII.
  10. Inferior alveolar nerve comes from the posterior division of CN V3.

2 · Commonly Compared Items

Danger zone of the scalp vs danger triangle of the face

🧢 Danger zone (scalp)

Where: SCALP layer 4, the loose areolar tissue

Scalp infection / blood→Emissary veins→Diploic veins→Dural venous sinuses

Risk: meningitis, sinus thrombosis, skull osteomyelitis.

Also: blood spreads freely in this layer. The frontal belly has no bony attachment, so blood tracks into the eyelids → bilateral black eyes.

👃 Danger triangle (face)

Where: corners of the mouth → bridge of the nose

Facial vein→Angular vein→Superior ophthalmic vein→Cavernous sinus
Deep route:Deep facial vein→Pterygoid venous plexus→Emissary veins→Cavernous sinus

Risk: cavernous sinus thrombosis. CN VI first (lateral gaze palsy), then CN III, IV, V1, V2. Proptosis, chemosis.

“Angry Superheroes Crash.”

Shared principle
Both routes use valveless veins, so blood and infection can flow backward into the skull.

High-yield
The lateral pterygoid defines the 3 parts of the maxillary artery: behind it (mandibular), on it (pterygoid), beyond it (pterygopalatine). It is the only muscle of mastication that opens the jaw. In anterior TMJ dislocation, it pulls the condyle and disc forward over the articular eminence; the closing muscles then spasm. Reduce by pressing down, then back.

Parotid high-yield
  • Contents superficial → deep: CN VII → retromandibular vein → external carotid artery.
  • Mumps: severe pain because the capsule is tough and can’t expand; chewing compresses the gland. Capsule sensation = great auricular nerve.

  • Stones favor the submandibular gland: thick, alkaline, calcium-rich saliva + long uphill duct.

12 · Self-Check Questions

Answer first, then open each one to check.

1. A pin is on the nerve emerging between the two heads of lateral pterygoid. Name it and its function.
Buccal nerve (anterior division of CN V3): sensory to cheek skin and mucosa. It does not move buccinator.
2. Which nerve carries parasympathetic fibers to the parotid gland, and from which ganglion?
Auriculotemporal nerve (CN V3), carrying postganglionic fibers from the otic ganglion (preganglionic: CN IX → lesser petrosal nerve).
3. The chorda tympani synapses in which ganglion?
Submandibular ganglion → submandibular and sublingual glands.
4. Name the three parts of the maxillary artery and the structure that defines them.
Mandibular, pterygoid, pterygopalatine — defined by the lateral pterygoid.
5. A pin is on a vein inside the parotid, behind the ramus. What two veins form it, and where does each division drain?
Retromandibular vein = superficial temporal + maxillary veins. Anterior division + facial vein → common facial → internal jugular vein. Posterior division + posterior auricular vein → external jugular vein.
6. Which muscle of facial expression is pierced by the parotid duct, and what supplies it?
Buccinator — buccal branch of CN VII.
7. Where is the mental foramen, and what passes through it?
Below the lower 2nd premolar; mental nerve and mental artery.
8. A pin is on the thin sheet of muscle over the neck. Group, arch and nerve?
Platysma — muscle of facial expression, arch 2, cervical branch of CN VII.
9. What layer of the scalp is the “dangerous layer,” and why?
Layer 4, loose areolar tissue — emissary veins connect it to the dural venous sinuses, so infection can spread intracranially.
10. The nerve to medial pterygoid also supplies which two muscles?
Tensor tympani and tensor veli palatini.
11. Which cranial nerve is usually affected first in cavernous sinus thrombosis, and why?
CN VI — it runs free through the sinus next to the internal carotid artery; the others are in the lateral wall.
12. Which branch of CN VII crosses superficial to the facial artery and vein at the lower border of the mandible?
Mandibular branch of CN VII.
