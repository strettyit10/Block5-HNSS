1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Write full names (“levator palpebrae superioris,” not “LPS”; “superior orbital fissure,” not “SOF”). Cranial nerves may be written as CN III or oculomotor nerve.
  • Spelling counts: trochlea · levator palpebrae superioris · sphincter pupillae · nasociliary · supratrochlear · infratrochlear · periorbita · puncta · canaliculi.
★ High-yield traps
  1. “LR6, SO4, AO3” — lateral rectus = CN VI, superior oblique = CN IV, all others (including LPS) = CN III.
  2. Lacrimal gland: CN VII → greater petrosal nerve → pterygopalatine ganglion → zygomatic nerve (V2) → lacrimal nerve (V1) → gland. The lesser petrosal nerve is CN IX → otic ganglion → parotid.
  3. Every orbital artery is a branch of the ophthalmic artery (from the internal carotid artery), and each travels with the nerve of the same name.
  4. Only parasympathetic fibers synapse in the ciliary ganglion. Short ciliary nerves come from the ganglion; long ciliary nerves come from the nasociliary nerve and bypass it.
  5. Light reflex: afferent CN II, efferent CN III. Corneal reflex: afferent CN V1 (nasociliary), efferent CN VII (bilateral).
  6. H-test: ABduct → test the Recti · ADduct → test the Obliques.
  7. Infratrochlear (V1, nasociliary) ≠ infraorbital (V2, orbital floor).
  8. The orbital apex is posteromedial — the optic canal is in the lesser wing of the sphenoid.

2 · Bony Orbit, Walls & Openings

Seven bones form the orbit: frontal, zygomatic, maxilla, lacrimal, ethmoid, sphenoid, palatine. It’s a four-sided pyramid: broad base at the orbital margin, apex at the optic canal (posteromedial). The eyeball fills only the anterior half; the posterior half is orbital fat, extraocular muscles, nerves and vessels. The periorbita is the periosteum lining the orbit.

3 · Lacrimal Apparatus

“Tears are made superolaterally and drained medially.”

Tear flow

Lacrimal gland→Lacrimal ducts→Lateral superior conjunctival fornix→Conjunctival sac (blinking spreads it)→Lacrimal puncta (on lacrimal papillae)→Lacrimal canaliculi→Lacrimal sac→Nasolacrimal duct→Inferior nasal meatus

Drain analogy

Puncta = drain holes · canaliculi = small pipes · lacrimal sac = collecting trap · nasolacrimal duct = main pipe → inferior meatus (why your nose runs when you cry).

Nearby surface structures

The lacrimal caruncle sits at the medial corner but is not part of the drainage pathway.

Secretomotor (parasympathetic) supply

CN VII→Greater petrosal nerve→Pterygopalatine ganglion (synapse)→Zygomatic nerve (V2)→communicating branchLacrimal nerve (V1)→Lacrimal gland
Greater vs lesser petrosal
Greater petrosal = Grief (tears), CN VII → pterygopalatine ganglion. Lesser petrosal = Lunch (parotid saliva), CN IX → otic ganglion.

4 · Extraocular Muscles

Seven voluntary muscles: 4 recti, 2 obliques, and levator palpebrae superioris. All but the inferior oblique arise near the orbital apex; the inferior oblique arises from the anterior orbital floor.

The H-test for one eye. Left bar = eye turned OUT (temporal); right bar = eye turned IN (nasal). Obliques act opposite to their names: inferior oblique elevates, superior oblique depresses.

Pairing rules

  • SR + IR both adduct
  • SO + IO both abduct
  • SR + SO both intort — “Superiors INtort”
  • IR + IO both extort

Why secondary actions?

The orbital axis angles posteromedially while the optical axis points straight ahead, so SR, IR and the obliques pull at an angle. Moving the eye into abduction or adduction lines a muscle up with its pull — that’s the H-test.
Conjugate gaze: looking to the right = right lateral rectus (CN VI) + left medial rectus (CN III).

6 · CN V1 in the Orbit — “NFL”

CN V1 (ophthalmic division) — enters through the superior orbital fissure

N · Nasociliary nerve

Crosses over CN II toward the medial wall

  • Anterior ethmoidal nerve → anterior ethmoidal foramen → ends as external nasal nerve (tip of nose)
  • Posterior ethmoidal nerve → posterior ethmoidal foramen
  • Infratrochlear nerve → medial corner of the eye (below trochlea)
  • Long ciliary nerves → eyeball (cornea), bypass the ganglion
  • Sensory root to the ciliary ganglion

F · Frontal nerve

Largest; on top of LPS along the roof

  • Supraorbital nerve (lateral) → supraorbital notch/foramen (frontal bone) → forehead, scalp, upper lid
  • Supratrochlear nerve (medial) → above the trochlea → medial forehead

L · Lacrimal nerve

Along the upper lateral wall

  • Sensory: lateral upper lid, conjunctiva, gland region
  • Receives the communicating branch from the zygomatic nerve (V2) → carries postganglionic secretomotor fibers to the lacrimal gland
  • Lab landmark: find the lacrimal gland first
Hutchinson sign
Herpes zoster vesicles on the tip of the nose = external nasal branch of the nasociliary nerve → high risk of ocular involvement, because the nasociliary nerve also supplies the eye (long ciliary nerves).

CN V2 in the orbit: the zygomatic nerve (→ zygomaticofacial + zygomaticotemporal) and infraorbital nerve enter through the inferior orbital fissure; the infraorbital nerve runs in the floor and exits the infraorbital foramen.

7 · Ciliary Ganglion & Ciliary Nerves

A tiny parasympathetic ganglion in the posterior orbit, lateral to the optic nerve. One of the four head ganglia — “COPS”: Ciliary, Otic, Pterygopalatine, Submandibular.

Veins

Superior ophthalmic vein → superior orbital fissure.
Inferior ophthalmic vein → inferior orbital fissure.
Both connect to the cavernous sinus — valveless, so infection can spread backward.

Not in the orbit

The facial artery is an external carotid branch on the face — it does not supply the orbit’s contents. Orbit = ophthalmic artery (internal carotid system).

11 · Self-Check Questions

Answer first, then open each one to check.

1. Which structures pass through the optic canal?
CN II and the ophthalmic artery.
2. Name the three V1 branches that enter through the superior orbital fissure.
Nasociliary, frontal and lacrimal nerves (“NFL”).
3. Which muscle elevates the eye when it is adducted?
Inferior oblique (CN III).
4. Which muscle depresses the eye when it is abducted?
Inferior rectus (CN III).
5. Trace the secretomotor pathway to the lacrimal gland.
CN VII → greater petrosal nerve → pterygopalatine ganglion → zygomatic nerve (V2) → lacrimal nerve (V1) → lacrimal gland.
6. Which fibers synapse in the ciliary ganglion, and what do they supply?
Parasympathetic fibers from CN III → sphincter pupillae and ciliary muscle.
7. Where do long ciliary nerves come from?
The nasociliary nerve (V1); they bypass the ciliary ganglion.
8. What artery gives rise to the supraorbital and lacrimal arteries?
The ophthalmic artery (from the internal carotid artery).
9. Through which openings do the superior and inferior ophthalmic veins pass?
Superior orbital fissure and inferior orbital fissure, respectively.
10. An orbital floor fracture sends contents into which space, and which nerve is at risk?
Maxillary sinus; infraorbital nerve (V2).
11. Which nerve lies just below the trochlea, and what is its parent?
Infratrochlear nerve — branch of the nasociliary nerve.
12. Which tear-drainage structures does fluid pass through between the puncta and the nasolacrimal duct?
Lacrimal canaliculi → lacrimal sac.
