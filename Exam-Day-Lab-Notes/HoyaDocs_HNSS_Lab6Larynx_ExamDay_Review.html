1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Full names. Spelling counts: arytenoid · corniculate · cuneiform · cricoid · cricothyroid · thyrohyoid · quadrangular membrane · conus elasticus · rima glottidis · infraglottic · piriform recess · vallecula · posterior cricoarytenoid.
  • “Vocal fold” = true cord; “vestibular fold” = false cord.
★ High-yield traps
  1. Posterior cricoarytenoid = the ONLY abductor of the vocal folds (“PCA = Pulls Cords Apart”) — recurrent laryngeal nerve.
  2. Cricothyroid = the only intrinsic muscle NOT supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve → external laryngeal nerve; tilts the thyroid cartilage → tenses the folds → raises pitch.
  3. The vocal fold is the dividing line: above = internal laryngeal nerve (sensory, cough reflex) + superior laryngeal artery + superior deep cervical nodes; below = recurrent laryngeal nerve + inferior laryngeal artery + inferior deep cervical / pretracheal / paratracheal nodes.
  4. Internal laryngeal nerve + superior laryngeal artery pierce the thyrohyoid membrane.
  5. Ventricle = space between the vestibular (false) and vocal (true) folds.
  6. Vocal fold = vocal ligament (upper edge of the conus elasticus) + vocalis + mucosa. Vestibular fold covers the vestibular ligament (lower edge of the quadrangular membrane).
  7. Epiglottis = elastic cartilage. Vallecula = oropharynx (anterior to epiglottis); piriform recess = laryngopharynx (lateral to the inlet).
  8. Cricothyroidotomy = through the cricothyroid ligament; tracheostomy = between tracheal rings.

4 · Laryngoscopic (Superior) View

View from above, as in laryngoscopy. The piriform recesses flank the aryepiglottic folds and funnel food around the inlet toward the esophagus.

Vallecula vs. piriform recess

Epiglottic valleculaBetween tongue base + epiglottis · oropharynx · laryngoscope blade sits here
Piriform recessLateral to the inlet/AE folds · laryngopharynx · food “gutters”; fish bones lodge here → internal laryngeal nerve at risk (under the mucosa)

Vocal fold position

  • Phonation: folds closed (adducted)
  • Respiration: folds open (abducted); wider in forced breathing

★ Vocal fold cancer
Lymphatics in the vocal folds are sparse → vocal fold cancers rarely metastasize to lymph nodes.

8 · Laryngopharynx (Hypopharynx) & Swallowing

Laryngopharynx

  • Most inferior part of the pharynx; opens into the larynx via the laryngeal inlet; continues into the esophagus
  • Wall: middle + inferior constrictors, stylopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus
  • Piriform recesses = gutters guiding food around the inlet into the esophagus
  • All pharyngeal muscles = CN X except stylopharyngeus (CN IX)

Zenker’s diverticulum

Inferior constrictor = thyropharyngeus + cricopharyngeus. Weak area between them = Killian’s triangle → herniation. Food enters the sac → dysphagia, halitosis, regurgitation, aspiration.

10 · Self-Check

1. Which muscle is the only abductor of the vocal folds, and what nerve supplies it?
Posterior cricoarytenoid — recurrent laryngeal nerve.
2. Which intrinsic laryngeal muscle is not supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve?
Cricothyroid — external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.
3. What pierces the thyrohyoid membrane?
Internal laryngeal nerve and superior laryngeal artery.
4. A fish bone lodged lateral to the laryngeal inlet damages which nerve? What is lost?
Internal laryngeal nerve (under the piriform recess mucosa) → loss of sensation above the vocal folds and the cough reflex.
5. What is the space between the vestibular and vocal folds?
The laryngeal ventricle (with the saccule producing mucus).
6. The vocal ligament is the upper border of which membrane?
Conus elasticus. (Vestibular ligament = lower border of the quadrangular membrane.)
7. After thyroid surgery a patient is hoarse. Which nerve, and which artery does it accompany?
Recurrent laryngeal nerve; inferior thyroid artery.
8. Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury is dangerous because…?
Both posterior cricoarytenoids are lost → folds can’t abduct → stridor/airway obstruction.
9. Where do lymphatics drain from below the vocal folds?
Inferior deep cervical, pretracheal and paratracheal nodes.
10. Why do vocal fold cancers rarely spread to nodes?
The vocal folds have sparse lymphatics.
11. What is the shape of the cricoid cartilage, and what sits on it?
Signet ring (tall posterior lamina); the arytenoid cartilages sit on its posterior lamina.
12. Name the borders of the laryngeal inlet.
Anterior: epiglottis; lateral: aryepiglottic folds; posterior: interarytenoid notch.
13. Which muscle lowers pitch?
Thyroarytenoid (relaxes/shortens the vocal folds).
14. Where is Killian’s triangle, and what forms there?
Between thyropharyngeus and cricopharyngeus (inferior constrictor); Zenker’s diverticulum.
