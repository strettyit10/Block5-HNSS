1 · Exam-Day Rules & Top Traps

✍️ Wording
  • Use anatomical directions: anterior/posterior, superior/inferior, medial/lateral.
  • Spelling counts: torus tubarius · salpingopharyngeal fold · salpingopharyngeus · piriform recess · palatoglossal / palatopharyngeal · cricopharyngeus · thyropharyngeus · epiglottic vallecula · choana(e).
★ High-yield traps
  1. All pharyngeal muscles = CN X (pharyngeal plexus) except stylopharyngeus = CN IX.
  2. Palatoglossus = CN X — the only tongue muscle not supplied by CN XII.
  3. The pharyngotympanic tube connects the middle ear to the nasopharynx; it is opened by tensor veli palatini (CN V3) and levator veli palatini (CN X).
  4. Pharyngeal recess (posterior to the torus tubarius) = classic site of nasopharyngeal carcinoma → serous otitis media + neck mass.
  5. CN IX lies deep to the tonsillar bed → tonsillectomy can cause loss of taste/sensation on the posterior 1/3 of the tongue.
  6. Epiglottis flips down over the laryngeal inlet; the vallecula is where the laryngoscope blade goes.
  7. Killian’s triangle (thyropharyngeus ↔ cricopharyngeus) → Zenker’s diverticulum.
  8. Laryngeal inlet = the opening; laryngopharynx = the region.

4 · Oropharynx

The “tonsil sandwich,” anterior → posterior.
  • Palatoglossal fold marks the transition from the oral cavity to the oropharynx.
  • Epiglottic vallecula = space between the epiglottis and the posterior 1/3 of the tongue — the laryngoscope blade goes here during intubation.
  • All palate muscles are CN X (pharyngeal plexus) except tensor veli palatini (CN V3).
Tonsillectomy
Removing the palatine tonsil risks CN IX, which lies deep to the tonsillar bed → temporary loss of taste and sensation on the posterior 1/3 of the tongue.

5 · Laryngopharynx (Hypopharynx)

Structures

  • Epiglottis — leaf-shaped; flips down over the laryngeal inlet in the pharyngeal phase
  • Laryngeal inlet (aditus) — opening into the larynx; epiglottis forms its anterior boundary
  • Piriform recesses — “gutters” on each side of the inlet that funnel food into the esophagus
  • Continues inferiorly as the esophagus

Wall

Mainly the inferior and middle constrictors, plus stylopharyngeus and palatopharyngeus.

Under the piriform mucosa: the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve (sensory above the vocal folds). A lodged foreign body (e.g., fish bone) can injure it → lost cough reflex, aspiration risk.

Trachea

C-shaped cartilage rings, open posteriorly so the esophagus behind it can expand when a bolus passes.

Esophagus

Posterior to the trachea. Its upper esophageal sphincter = cricopharyngeus (lowest part of the inferior constrictor).

Beyond lecture (enrichment): tumors at each level threaten a different nerve — nasopharynx → skull-base group (superior cervical ganglion, CN XII); oropharynx / tonsil → CN IX (referred ear pain); laryngopharynx / piriform recess → CN X (hoarseness, aspiration). Going down the pharynx, the CN numbers go up.

10 · Self-Check Questions

Answer first, then open each one to check.

1. Name the lateral-wall structures of the nasopharynx, anterior → posterior.
Choana → opening of the pharyngotympanic tube → torus tubarius (salpingopharyngeal fold inferiorly) → pharyngeal recess.
2. What does the pharyngotympanic tube connect, and which muscles open it?
Middle ear ↔ nasopharynx; tensor veli palatini (CN V3) and levator veli palatini (CN X).
3. What are the boundaries of the oropharynx?
Soft palate to the superior border of the epiglottis.
4. What lies between the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal folds?
The tonsillar fossa containing the palatine tonsil (CN IX deep to its bed).
5. What is the function of the piriform recesses?
Gutters that direct food/drink into the esophagus, away from the larynx.
6. Which pharyngeal muscle is not supplied by CN X?
Stylopharyngeus — CN IX.
7. Name the two parts of the inferior constrictor and the weak area between them.
Thyropharyngeus and cricopharyngeus; Killian’s triangle (→ Zenker’s diverticulum).
8. What passes through the gap between the middle and inferior constrictors?
Internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve + superior laryngeal artery (through the thyrohyoid membrane).
9. What happens in the oropharyngeal phase of swallowing?
Nasopharynx sealed off, larynx elevated, pharynx widened.
10. Where is nasopharyngeal carcinoma classically found, and what are its signs?
Pharyngeal recess; serous otitis media + neck mass.
11. Why are tracheal rings open posteriorly?
So the esophagus posterior to the trachea can expand during swallowing.
12. Which retropharyngeal nerve stays in the carotid sheath the whole length of the neck?
CN X (vagus).
